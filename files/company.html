Balance should be measurable, objective and accessible.

Balance Mat turns the quiet, constant sway of the human body into meaningful data — giving researchers and clinicians an objective read on postural stability, for the first time at scale.

Every milestone has been driven by the same goal: turning complex human movement into meaningful, measurable information.

2016

Balance Mat established

Research and development of optical fibre technology for healthcare applications begins.

2017

Early balance testing

Prototype Balance Mat technology is tested with older adults through collaboration with the Council on the Ageing.

2019

Technology validation

The Multimetric Balance Mat is validated against force-plate technology through RMIT University.

2020

Australian patent granted

The company's Balance Mat technology receives Australian patent protection.

2021

Neurometric development and validation

The Neurometric Balance Mat is developed and subsequently validated through collaboration with the University of Canberra.

2021

ARTG listing

Balance Mat is included in the Australian Register of Therapeutic Goods (ARTG).

2022

Market launch

The Balance Mat system officially enters the market.

2022 onward

Balance Mat systems continue to be developed for healthcare, allied health, research and specialised applications, while partnerships and research programs expand.

Advancing balance measurement

Our mission

Making balance measurable. Making insight accessible.

Our mission is to transform the way human balance is measured and understood. We combine innovative technology, research and collaboration to provide objective insights into postural stability — helping researchers and healthcare professionals better understand balance and its role in human health.

We believe better measurement can lead to better understanding, better decisions and better outcomes.
Our vision

A world where balance is understood before it becomes a problem.

We envision a future where objective balance assessment is accessible across healthcare, research and community settings. From clinical research to population health, we see opportunities to identify meaningful changes in balance earlier, monitor them over time, and unlock new insights into human health.

Make objective balance measurement part of the future of healthcare.
